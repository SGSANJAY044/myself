---
title: Why I'm doing this
---

I've been coding long enough that it stopped feeling like anything.

It used to. I remember staying up after hackathons too wired to sleep, feeling like we'd built something real in a night. Even when what we built already existed and we just pitched it better than the other teams.

Then I got a job. Then AI tools got good. Now I can ship features faster than ever and I care about them less than ever. That's a weird place to be.

A few days ago I read [Alex West's post](https://alexwest.co/book-one) about building a bunch of side projects until one of them became a real business. What got me wasn't the money part. It was that he just kept trying small things and didn't treat any single one like it had to be the one.

That's what I want to do.

## the rules (for now)

- **Keep it short.** A few weeks per project, max. If it's not getting anywhere, I kill it and write about why.
- **Think like a builder, not a dev.** The code is the easy part now. I want to learn the stuff I've been avoiding: who's going to pay, how they find it, why they'd stay.
- **Write it all down.** Especially the mistakes. I'm going to make plenty.

I don't know if I'm good at this. I've never actually tried. That's the honest version.

First project is this site. Next one, I'll figure out this week.
